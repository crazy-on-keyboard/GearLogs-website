Guide 4.9 · Settings

Look after your own sign-in

Every member looks after their own sign-in. Change your password, check your two-factor, and see what GearLogs tells you when your account is used.

Before you start

  • Settings › Security opens for every member. Everything on it is about your own sign-in.
  • To set a new password there, you need your current one.
  • Forgot it? On the sign-in screen, press Forgot password?. GearLogs emails you an 8-digit code. You type it in the same tab, with your new password. The code works for one hour and only once. If your login uses two-factor, you confirm it before the password changes.
  • One account is active in one place at a time. Signing in somewhere else signs this place out, on a screen headed Signed in somewhere else.

  1. Open Settings › Security.

    Open Settings from the side menu, then Security in its list.

    1. Settings in the side menu.
    2. Security in the Settings list.
    3. Change password: a new password for your own login.
    4. Your two-factor: a 6-digit code on top of your password.

    Administrators also see Workspace two-factor and Support views · last 90 days further down. Guides 4.15 and 4.21 cover them.

  2. Fill in the three boxes.

    Change password asks for your current password first, then the new one twice.

    1. Current password: the one you sign in with today.
    2. New password and Confirm new password: type the new one twice.
    3. The three rules: At least 12 characters, Letters and numbers, Both entries match. Each rule turns green with a tick once it holds.

    Show inside a box shows what you typed. In New password, it shows both new boxes at once.

  3. Change the password and sign in again.

    Before you press, the card tells you what happens.

    1. The card’s promise: every device is signed out, this one included, and you get a confirmation email.
    2. The same warning, right above the button.
    3. Change password. You can press it once the three rules hold and the current password is in.

    Every device signed in to your account then shows Password changed, this one included. Press Sign in here instead and sign in with the new password. Got that email but did not change your password? Use Forgot password? right away and tell your administrator.

  4. Check your two-factor.

    Your two-factor shows where you stand.

    1. The title and your status: Not set up, Set up — verify to manage, or Verified this session.
    2. What two-factor is: a 6-digit code from your authenticator, on top of your password.
    3. The button follows your status: Set up my two-factor, Verify my two-factor, or New recovery codes.

    Guide 4.15 walks through setting it up. New recovery codes shows a fresh set of ten at once, and your old codes stop working. Save them, then press I’ve saved them.

  5. See what you are told about your account.

    Settings › Notifications has a Security section under My notifications.

    1. Security: the section about your own account.
    2. New sign-in to my account: a note each time your account signs in afresh. One switch for the app, one for email.
    3. Password, two-factor and recovery changes: Always on, in the app and by email. Nobody can switch it off.

    A switch saves the moment you flip it. A password change always sends a note, whoever made it: you, a reset code, or an administrator. A note you did not expect means someone else changed it. Administrators also see A new administrator is added, always on. Guide 4.18 covers the rest of the list.

Result

Your new password works on every device you sign in to again. The two-factor card shows where you stand, and GearLogs tells you whenever your password, two-factor or recovery codes change.

If something is refused

  • That is not your current password. The current password box empties. Type it again.
  • Enter your current password first. Shown beside the button when the new password is ready but the current one is empty.
  • That is already your password — choose a new one. The new password is the same as the current one. Nothing changed.
  • That password is too easy to guess — choose a longer one. GearLogs refused it, even though the three rules hold. Pick a longer one.
  • Too many attempts — try again in a few minutes. Nothing changed. Wait, then try again.
  • That code is not right or has expired. Check the email, or request a new code. On the reset screen. Request a new code takes you back one step to send a fresh one.
