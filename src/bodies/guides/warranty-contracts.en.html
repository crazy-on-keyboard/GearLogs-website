Guide 4.11 · Registry

Track warranties and service contracts

Record your service contracts, attach them to units and lots, and see every warning before a warranty runs out.

Before you start

  • Recording, editing and archiving contracts, attaching gear and giving a unit its own warranty date need the right Create/Edit Items. Administrators and operators have it.
  • Everyone who can open the Registry sees the Contracts tab. Without that right it has no New contract, no Cost column and no buttons.
  • Contracts cover units. The Registry shows them once at least one item is tracked by unit.
  • The warning threshold in Settings is set by an administrator.

  1. Open the Contracts tab.

    Open Registry from the side menu, then Contracts in the band.

    1. Contracts in the band.
    2. New contract opens the form for a new contract.
    3. The chip under Ends: the days left. Green while the end is far off, amber inside the warning window you set in step 7, red with EXPIRED once it has passed.
    4. The row’s buttons: Attach gear, Edit and Archive.

    Each row shows the provider, the coverage, how much gear the contract covers and, for those who hold Create/Edit Items, the cost. Click a row to see the gear it covers underneath. The search box above the table filters contracts by code, provider, contract number or covered gear.

  2. Record a contract.

    New contract opens New Contract.

    1. Provider, which is required, and the Contract #.
    2. Coverage: REPAIR, REPLACE, FULL SERVICE, PARTS ONLY or OTHER. Cost beside it.
    3. Starts, Ends and Purchased. Only Ends is required.
    4. Save creates the contract.

    Notes takes up to 500 characters. GearLogs gives every contract its own CT code. Only people who hold Create/Edit Items receive the cost. Edit on the row changes the contract later. Archive asks first: the contract stops counting toward warranty coverage, and its gear comes off it. It then waits in Archive › Contract Archives, and a restore brings it back with no gear attached.

  3. Attach gear to the contract.

    Attach gear on the contract’s row opens Attach gear for that contract.

    1. Units or Lot: attach single units, or whole lots.
    2. The filter box: find gear by name, serial number or code.
    3. COVERED: this unit is already on the contract.
    4. Attach puts the unit on the contract at once.

    A lot covers every unit in it, now and later. Each click attaches one unit or one lot, without a question. The list holds only units in stock, issued, in transit or in repair. To take gear off a contract, open its row in the table: Detach beside the unit asks first.

  4. Check one unit’s coverage.

    In the Property Book, pick the item, then Coverage on a unit’s row. Coverage opens.

    1. A contract that covers this unit, by its code and provider.
    2. Detach takes the unit off that contract. It asks first.
    3. Attach to contract: pick another contract.
    4. Attach adds it.

    A contract that reaches the unit through its lot shows via lot, and comes off at the lot or in the contract itself. A lot’s own row in the Property Book has the same Coverage button.

  5. Give one unit its own warranty date.

    Warranty on the same row opens Unit warranty.

    1. Current: the date that applies now, and where it comes from: unit override, from a service contract or from the lot.
    2. Warranty until (override): a date for this unit only, after a repair or a replacement, say.
    3. Save sets it.

    The unit’s own date comes first. Without one, the later date wins: the lot’s Warranty until (set in Add Lot or Edit Lot) or the latest end date of the contracts that cover the unit, whichever is later. A contract extends a warranty; it never shortens one. Saving with the date emptied clears the override, and the unit inherits again. Units that are written off, lost or retired raise no warnings.

  6. Find what is running out.

    The Expiring count at the top of the Registry counts the units whose warranty, or whose lot’s expiry date, is inside the warning window or already past. Click it.

    1. Expiring: how many units need attention.
    2. Clear the expiring filter: the Ledger now shows only those units. This button shows them all again.
    3. Warranty: each unit’s chip. EXP marks a lot’s expiry date.

    The same units show elsewhere. On the Logistics board, an item with such units carries a clock among its icons: amber, and red once a date has passed. On the dashboard, the card Expiring warranties & contracts lists them, the most urgent first.

  7. Choose when the warnings begin.

    Open Settings from the side menu, then Organization Setup.

    1. Settings in the side menu.
    2. Organization Setup.
    3. Expiry warning threshold: how many days before a date the warnings begin, from 1 to 365. It starts at 30.

    The number saves when you leave the field. A number outside the range is brought inside it. Every chip, count, clock and card follows it at once, for everyone.

Result

Every unit shows which warranty date applies and where it comes from. As a date comes near, the Registry, the board and the dashboard warn you together, from the same day.

If something is refused

  • Required You pressed Save with Provider or Ends empty. Nothing was saved. Fill it in and save again.
  • The end date can’t be before the start date Ends is earlier than Starts. Correct one of the two dates.
  • Already covered by this contract Someone attached the same unit or lot a moment before you. The list refreshes and shows it as COVERED.
  • No active contracts — create one in the Contracts view. The workspace has no contract outside the archive, so Coverage has nothing to offer. Record one in Contracts first.
