Field Guide

Guides

Short, practical walkthroughs for getting things done in GearLogs.

4.1

Set Up Your Board

Create a tab for each department, add groups inside it, and place your items or people. Every tab and group carries a permanent reference code and its own colour. A Logistics group card shows, above its list, a ring of stock split into in stock, in use and in repair, a column chart of the last 30, 60 or 90 days of activity, and who currently holds the group’s gear, by department. Edit Layout lets you move a group earlier or later and set how many columns it spans — groups stack in independent columns, so a short group never leaves a gap under it — while how many groups sit across a row is your own display setting. Reorder tabs from a tab’s Edit dialog; drag a card to another group or tab and confirm the move.

4.2

Assign & Return Equipment

Assign items with quantities — open the person, or use the quick Assign button right on an item’s card and pick the person from a searchable list. Process returns when equipment comes back. Stock adjusts automatically and every step is recorded. The same small card goes further when you need it: ↗ More people on an item’s Assign card opens the two-pane hand-out window pre-filled with what you typed — tick a whole group, give each person their own quantity, and see what each will hold afterwards and what is left; ↗ More items on a person’s card opens the same window with your tabs, groups and every item in stock on one side and a row per item on the other. The maths comes first: a row that asks for more than is in stock turns red and says how many are short, and nothing is handed out until the list fits. Serial-numbered items deal their units in the window, the oldest serviceable first; a unit that needs repair is never dealt automatically. A hand-out is refused by name, with the same sentence wherever you try it, when the item is sensitive and you lack the right, or when the person or the item has been archived; in the window a sensitive item you may not hand out stays visible, greyed, with a small (i) that says why.

4.3

Sign Out for a Team

A department head can sign out a batch of gear in one step and hold it all under their name. Issue Batch on the head’s card opens the hand-out window with its own tool inside: tick items, groups or whole tabs from the tree, type a quantity per item or take All in stock for one row or every row, watch the running line, and confirm once. After the act a manifest opens for printing; the head signs the whole batch on My Gear as one receipt.

4.4

Build Your Dashboard

Add Widget opens a gallery of ready-made widgets — activity by type or by user, items by group, people by department, units by status, capacity fill, headroom, expiring, write-offs, kit lines out (as a number or per person), kits issued vs returned, receipts by state, who is waiting for a signature, time to confirm, opened vs confirmed and more — pick one, name it, done. Or Build your own: choose a source (Inventory Items, Personnel, Activity Logs, Write-offs, Registry, Kits, Approvals or Board shortcuts), answer plain questions — count what, filtered how, grouped by what — and pick only a visual that can honestly show it, with a live preview as you go. Activity, write-off and kits-issued-vs-returned widgets follow the dashboard period or own one, from 7 days to all time; trend charts group by day, week or month; lists show every row or the top 3, 5, 12 or 25; every timed card states the window it shows. Size any widget on a grid up to four columns wide and two rows tall; a card keeps its size and a long list scrolls inside it. Each widget says who sees it — everyone, administrators or only you. The Approvals cards are counted on the server: a member who holds only View Own Analytics sees the receipts they recorded on three of the four, captioned Receipts you recorded; the Waiting for a signature card lists everyone’s receipts and is not available to them.

4.5

Control Who Has Access

Give each member a role, or fine-tune exactly what they can view, edit, and manage. Members are listed by name, with an optional ID field of your own you can name to suit your organisation. Everyone sees only the areas they're meant to — and permanently erasing records is reserved for a Super Administrator, so nothing important is deleted by accident. Any right except Manage Users can also be granted to a person on top of their role: the checklist in Users › Edit marks what is “Included in this role” and what is administrators-only, and a role’s own rights are never taken away. Two rights meet kits: Manage kits (administrators and operators) builds a kit and its lines; handing a kit out or taking it back is the same Assign/Return Assets right as any hand-out, and a loss recorded inside a return needs Write Off / Report Lost Gear. Staff contact details — phone, email, notes — are a right of their own (“View contact details”), held by administrators and operators by default; members without it see a small lock in place of the contact markers, the workspace itself withholds the data, and whoever cannot see a detail can never overwrite it. Every change a member makes is checked by the workspace itself against their acceptance of the current Terms of Service and Privacy Policy. You cannot change your own role while you are the only administrator — the field is greyed with the reason; with another administrator in place, giving up the administrator role asks before it saves.

4.6

Review Your History

Use the history button on any person, group, kit or tab — and History on an archived item in the Archive — to see everything that’s happened — assignments, returns, dispensing — in the same table System Logs uses: timestamp, user, type and action, the header staying put as you scroll, colour-coded and naming who did what, going back twelve months.

4.7

Retire People & Write Off Gear

When equipment is lost, damaged, or never comes back, write it off — the count drops and the loss is recorded. To retire someone who left with their gear, write off everything they hold and archive them in one step. Find retired people, archived equipment, and every write-off together in the Archive, and restore or recover anything from there. Archived records reach only members who can open the Archive — search follows the same rule, so nobody is offered a record they cannot open — and renaming a location, rank or department reaches archived records too, so nothing comes back under a stale name. Losses are flagged right on the person and item cards — open one to see its full loss history — and loss dashboards show the trends by reason, team, and item.

4.8

Track Serial Numbers in the Registry

Turn on serial tracking for an item, then register its units one at a time, in bulk, or by importing a spreadsheet. Group units into lots, assign a specific unit to a person, and move or send a unit for repair from its menu — every hand-off is recorded for you. Sending a unit for repair asks for a reason, and the reason travels with the unit into its history.

4.9

Look After Your Own Sign-In

Every member manages their own sign-in from Settings › Security: change a password (the current one is asked for first), set up two-factor, and keep recovery codes. A forgotten password is recovered from the sign-in screen with a one-time code sent by email. A password change signs out every device, this one included — each lands on a screen that says so, and you sign in again with the new password.

4.10

Manage Your Subscription and Seats

Administrators run billing from Settings › Billing. Subscribe to the base plan — $199 a month (or $1,990 a year) for five accounts with every capability — and pay by card in Paddle’s secure window; the workspace is active the moment the payment is confirmed. Beyond the five included accounts, each person who can sign in is a flat $39 a month ($390 a year); Priority Operations, a faster first reply within 4 business hours, is an optional add-on at $99 a month ($990 a year). Expansion seats and Priority Operations are added on the same screen right after subscribing, and the exact amount is shown before you confirm — adding bills the prorated remainder now, and removing or reducing settles at your next renewal. An account is a person who can sign in; disabling someone frees their seat at once, and lowering the count takes it off your next bill. Yearly is two months free. Email packs are added on the same screen: each pack is 1,000 emails a month for $5 a month ($48 a year on a yearly plan) on the same subscription, added now and billed for the rest of the period, and removed from your next bill. Cancel there too: access runs to the end of the paid period, and one click resumes before then. Invoices list on the screen; the PDF and your payment method open at Paddle. If a payment fails, you are told in the app and by email while it retries. Money coming back has its own lines in the same log — a refund requested, issued, declined or reversed, and a credit issued or reversed — because Paddle reviews a refund before it pays out.

4.11

Track Warranty and Service Contracts

Put a warranty date on a lot, or on a single unit when it differs — after a repair, say — and record service contracts on the Registry’s Contracts tab: provider, contract number, coverage type, dates, notes and cost. Attach a contract to individual units or to a whole lot; a unit’s Warranty dialog always shows which date applies and where it comes from. As dates approach they change colour across the app, the Registry’s Expiring count jumps to the units that need attention, the board shows a clock on the product, and the Expiring widget lists the most urgent first. You choose how many days ahead the warnings begin, in Organization Setup; cost is visible to managers only.

4.12

Reports, Exports and Backups

Data Ops › Exports holds fourteen ready-made reports across logistics, personnel, the registry, the archive, activity and approvals — from the Full Inventory Audit, Current Hand-outs, Kit Contents and Kit Issues & Returns to the Serial-Unit Register, Contracts & Coverage, the Write-offs Ledger and the Full Activity Log — and, under Approvals Reports, Hand-over receipts (one row per receipt: who it was for, its state, when it was opened and decided, and by whom — never the notes) and Receipt lines (one row per frozen line). Each downloads as CSV or a real Excel workbook, or prints as a proper report page (the reliable route to a PDF, Hebrew included), and reads your complete records. The same three buttons sit on the activity log, the archive tabs and the billing log, exporting the view as you filtered it. A Super Administrator can take an encrypted whole-workspace backup from Data Ops › Maintenance at any time; only your passphrase opens it. Exporting keeps working even if your subscription lapses. The Personnel Roster report carries a Notes column beside Phone and Email when the person running it holds “View contact details”; without that right the three columns are not there. The same right decides whether Hand-over receipts carries the person’s email column.

4.13

Find Anything

The search box at the top of every screen finds items, people, departments, groups, team members (administrators only), units, lots, service contracts and archived records — by name, reference code, serial or lot code, location, designation, department, manufacturer, model, category, SKU, GTIN, email, phone and the notes you wrote. Type a fragment: the board behind the box thins to the matches while the results group by type, best first, with the matched text highlighted. Click a result to go straight to it; on items, people, units, lots and contracts, a group with more than five matches ends in “Show all 12 items”, which carries your search onto that screen. Moving to another screen clears the box, so a stale search never follows you. A result whose name does not contain your word says where it matched — “Matched on notes”, “Matched on location”.

4.14

Make GearLogs Yours

Settings › Display is yours: text size (1–10), how many groups sit across a board row (1–6), whether action buttons are always shown or appear on hover, how the side menu rests — an icon rail whose names fold away once you pick a screen, or open all day — one of five themes — Spec-Sheet, Light Mode, Dark Mode, Tactical (Army) and Clinical (Medical) — and your language, English or Hebrew, which also turns the whole app right-to-left and sets the language of your emails. Theme and language are stored on your account and follow you to every device; the other four stay with the browser you set them in. Nothing you choose repaints a colleague’s screen. Administrators set the workspace defaults everyone starts from (the menu has none — it starts as the rail for everyone), and “Use workspace defaults” takes you back to them in one confirmed click. Theme and language also switch from the header, without opening Settings. Every screen opens the same way: its name and its (i) on one side, that screen’s tabs and buttons on the other; inside Settings every card opens with a title bar and every section with its own grey band; and in Spec-Sheet and Light Mode the accent is a slate blue, so a button that goes forward never looks like one that removes.

4.15

Turn On Two-Factor Sign-In

From Settings › Security, “Set up my two-factor” shows a QR code for your authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Authy and 1Password all work), asks for the six-digit code, then shows ten single-use recovery codes — copy them now, they are never shown again. From then on the card tells you where you stand — Not set up, Set up — verify to manage, or Verified this session — and “New recovery codes” replaces the set at any time. An administrator who has verified their own two-factor can require it for the whole workspace with “Turn on”: every member is asked to set it up at their next sign-in, and from then on every change needs a two-factor-verified sign-in — reading is never blocked once you are in. Lost your device? “Use a recovery code” at sign-in clears the old authenticator and walks you into a new one; administrators can also reset a locked-out member’s two-factor from the Users screen. Turning two-factor on or off is noted in the bell whichever way it happened, and an administrator’s reset says so once. If several wrong recovery codes are tried on an account in a short time, the account and every administrator of the workspace are told, by member code — nothing is locked, and a wrong code is still simply a wrong code.

4.16

Get Support Inside the App

Open Support in the sidebar and press New request: choose Question, Problem, Feature idea or Feedback, add a subject and the details (a Problem can point at a specific item), and send — you get a permanent reference such as SUP-014. Each request is a two-sided conversation with GearLogs Support; a reply reaches you in the bell, by email with a link straight back, and as a badge on the sidebar’s Support entry until you open it. Mark a request resolved yourself, or reopen it — sending a new message on a resolved request reopens it too. Administrators can switch the list from My requests to Workspace to see every request their team filed. A message holds up to 4,000 characters: the counter beside the box turns amber as you near the limit and red past it, with a line that says by how much — nothing you pasted is ever cut, and Send waits until it fits. Attachments are not accepted yet; quote reference codes instead. We usually reply within one business day.

4.17

Set a Storage Capacity

Give an item a Storage capacity in its form and a small meter appears beside its STOCK badge — green for what is in stock, blue for what is out and gold for what is in repair; everything you are responsible for counts, and written-off gear frees the room. Adding stock or registering units past the ceiling is never refused: the app asks “Record it anyway?” with the exact numbers, and records it if you say so. The line is crossed only above the capacity, and the meter turns red when it is. Capacity, held and headroom appear in the Full Inventory Audit and in three ready-made widgets — Capacity fill, Headroom and Over capacity — while the health check on Data Ops › Overview counts the items that are over. Administrators hear the moment an item goes over, once per crossing. Leave the field blank and there is no ceiling, no meter and no checks.

4.18

Choose What You Are Told About

Settings › Notifications has two columns, In-app and Email, and a row for each kind of notice. Everyone chooses their own: gear assigned to me, my return recorded, gear in my custody written off, a new sign-in to my account. Below them sit the always-on rows everyone gets: password, two-factor and recovery changes, and replies on your support requests — including a reminder if you leave one unopened. Administrators see two more optional rows — an item goes over its storage capacity, an item runs low — and two always-on rows of their own: a new administrator added, and payments, invoices, seats and subscription changes. Untouched rows follow the workspace default an administrator sets (in-app on, email off out of the box), and “Use workspace defaults” puts your own choices back in one click. In-app notices land in the header bell — a dash there means the count could not be checked yet — and the full history lives under Support › Notifications. Emails arrive in your own language.

A Daily digest row sits in My notifications: switch it on and the gear and stock emails you have chosen arrive as one morning message at your workspace’s morning hour instead of one email per event; in-app notices and the always-on rows are never held, and an administrator can make the digest the workspace default. Administrators also see the day’s email allowance at the top of the page: how many emails this workspace sent today and, when the allowance is used up, that routine notices are reaching people in the app instead — the person still hears, and administrators are told once a day how many notices went in-app only.

Delivery times opens each card. In Workspace default an administrator sets the workspace’s Time zone — Use it takes the browser’s — and the Morning hour, from 05:00 to 11:00, that both morning emails follow: the members’ digest and the morning mail to your people on My Gear. Quiet hours hold routine email until the window ends (22:00 to 06:00 to start); notices in the app still arrive at once, and security, billing and support emails never wait. Hours inside the quiet hours are greyed, and the reason is written under the row. Under My notifications each member sets Digest at for their own digest and chooses Workspace, Off or My own quiet hours.

Your workspace also has a monthly allowance — up to 5,000 emails a month included. Administrators see it as one meter under the banner: how much of the month you have used and when it resets. When the month is what stops your mail, the banner says so and offers Add a pack: packs of 1,000 emails a month ($5 a month, or $48 a year on a yearly plan) are added from Settings › Billing and removed any time. While your month’s allowance is used up, individual routine notices reach people in the app and go out by email when the month turns or a pack is added, as long as they are less than 14 days old — older ones stay as the in-app notice the person already has, and a daily digest at the ceiling is not resent because its items are already in the app. Your security, billing and support emails are never held by the month’s allowance. Every door that sends notices — hand-outs, returns, sign-outs, kits — shows you how many emails are left this month.

4.19

Import from a Spreadsheet

A preview shows how the file was read before anything is written, so you can check it against what you meant to import. Quantity and capacity columns take whole numbers only. Rows held back land in the conflicts queue with their reason attached — the reason reads in your language — and the clean rows still import. The staff CSV is different: a rule-breaking row refuses the whole file by row and field before any record is written.

4.20

How Dialogs Work

Every dialog in GearLogs is built the same way: a title bar with a close button, the content, and one shelf of buttons underneath — Cancel and the main action at the end, Archive or Delete in red text at the start, a single Close on dialogs that only show things. A form judges nothing while you type: a field turns red only after Save refuses it, a plain line under it says why, and a banner counts the fields to fix and takes you to the first. When an action cannot be done — a person still holding gear, the last administrator, a location still in use — the dialog stays open with the reason in red and the button greyed. Every dialog is announced by name to a screen reader and returns focus to where you were when it closes. An edited item or person form asks “Discard your changes?” before Escape, the close button or Cancel closes it; an untouched form closes at once.

4.21

See When Support Looked In

A support person never signs in as you. When you ask for help, they can open a read-only view of your workspace for a set number of minutes — it runs in their own tab under a red strip, every save is refused, and it ends by itself when the minutes run out. Afterwards the Activity Log shows a line for it (who viewed, as what role, why), and Settings › Security › Support views keeps the last ninety days with the reason and the minutes, so an administrator can always answer “who looked, and when”.

4.22

Hand Out a Kit

A kit is a named recipe of equipment — each item and how many of it per kit — with its own KT- code. Logistics switches between Equipment and Kits; the Kits area has its own tabs and cards, and + KIT and LINE build one line by line (serialized, consumable or plain items alike) for anyone with the Manage kits right. ISSUE opens two panes: Who receives it — a whole Personnel tab, groups, people, or a group minus a few names, found by search and ticked in one go; each tab and group folds, and a search opens the ones that hold a hit — beside What will happen: needed against in stock for every line, which serial unit each person gets (oldest in stock, changeable per person) and how many people will be told. Nothing is issued while a line is short; the short lines are named in red. Every line lands on the person’s card under the kit as one row — the kit’s colour, name and code, when it was issued, how many lines are still out and the next promised date — that opens to every line and its state, with the items handed out singly listed below; each person is told once, and the issue is kept with its names and quantities frozen even if the kit changes later. The dashboard’s Kits source counts the lines still out and draws issued against returned; Kit Contents and Kit Issues & Returns under Data Ops › Exports list every kit and every kit line ever issued.

4.23

Take a Kit Back

The small people button beside the history button on the kit card — it appears once the kit has been issued to anyone — opens Who holds this kit — the people tree with a count per tab and group and a search on the left (All people at the top; a holder who has since left the board under Not on the board), and on the right a table of every hand-out: who, when, the state, what is still out and the promised date, what came back, what was lost, damaged or consumed — and Return on a row that still has something out opens Return kit: the lines on the left, one decision at a time on the right; every line starts as Returned; per line choose Later (out until a promised date, which is required, with a note), Lost or Damaged (the reason, the date and a note go to the write-off ledger under your name); consumables are used up and do not come back. A refused save marks every line that still needs a word in the list, and Go to the first lands on its field. A write-off always asks first. The same dialog opens from the person’s card: Return on the kit’s row takes back that person’s lines. The card’s OPEN plate counts what is still out, the person’s card, the ledger and the Activity Log read what happened, and a line returned from the person’s card or the Registry closes the same way.

4.24

Open My Gear

You need three things: the workspace code your administrator gives you (it looks like TN-001), your ID number or personal number as it is on your record, and the email on your record. At my.gearlogs.com enter the three and press Send me a code; the page always says the same thing — if this record exists, a code is on its way — so nobody can learn from it whether a record exists. The code has eight characters, works for thirty minutes and only once; type it as written in the email — dashes and spaces do not matter — and press Sign in. Send a new code after a minute; a new code cancels the previous one. Tick Remember this device for 30 days on your own device only; any other device signs out after twelve hours. If the page asks you to press the button once more, a human-verification check on your connection needed a second look. Inside: My gear (each item, its code, the quantity, the serial and the day it was recorded), Notices (unread until you open them; opening one marks it read), History (received, used up, returned, written off, with who recorded it), and Approvals, marked Soon. The HE / EN switch is yours alone. Sign out ends this device; Sign out everywhere asks first and then ends every device you signed in on. If your workspace is on hold, the page says so, and you can still sign out.

4.25

Give Your People My Gear

Open the person’s card and fill two fields beside the contact details: ID number or Personal number (either one lets them sign in; spaces and dashes are ignored, and no two people in a workspace can share one) and the email on their record — both are contact details, so only roles with View contact details see or edit them, and the roster export carries them with the contacts. Tell them the workspace code. From then on they see their own gear, notices and history; a notice reaches them at the same three moments a member is told — gear assigned to them, their return recorded, an item on their name written off. Archive the person and their sign-in ends the same minute; change their email and every open session ends. If someone enters a wrong code five times in an hour, your administrators are told in the bell, and if your workspace uses up its day’s code allowance, the bell says so — no further codes go out until the next day. A person without an email on their record cannot receive a code; the storeman’s record stays the record for them.

4.26

Signing a Hand-over in My Gear

When gear is handed to a person on your list — or taken back, or written off from their name — a receipt appears in Approvals on their My Gear page, counted in the rail. They open it, read the lines as they were recorded, and either sign — I received these (or I returned these, or That’s right for a write-off), then Confirm and sign — or choose Something is wrong: a reason from the list (I did not receive it, Wrong item, Wrong quantity, Damaged, Other), a tick on the lines it is about, and optional words for your administrators; Other needs the words. Tell your people three things: a signature cannot be undone; a dispute is final for them — a member of your team closes it with them; a receipt nobody signs expires after 14 days and you hear about it in the bell. The gear itself never waits for the signature — the storeman’s record moved it the moment it was made.

4.27

Approvals — Reading a Receipt

Every hand-out, return and write-off to a person on your list opens a receipt the person signs on My Gear within 14 days; Approvals is where administrators see them all. The rail lists six states with live counts — Waiting, Disputed, Signed, On behalf, Cancelled, Expired — and a receipt opens to its facts, its lines frozen as they were handed over, and a timeline: opened, the morning mail queued, sent, delivered or not delivered, disputed with the person’s reason, signed, confirmed on behalf or cancelled by name.

Three actions sit under an open receipt for administrators and holders of the right Confirm a hand-over on behalf: Confirm on behalf closes it as received, Cancel receipt closes one recorded in error — both need a reason of at least 8 characters that stays with the receipt and is never mailed or logged — and Resend reminder sends the person a reminder now, once per person a day — during quiet hours it is refused and says when they end. Show notes opens the words behind a dispute or a decision, on a click, for those same people. The same state shows as a small mark on the person’s card, the item’s card and a kit’s row, and the mark opens the receipt. A person My Gear cannot sign in — no email on record, or neither an ID number nor a personal number — carries a No code mark on Personnel, with a filter to list them; add the missing detail on the person’s card and the mark clears.

On the dashboard, the Approvals source gives four cards — Receipts by state, Waiting for a signature, Time to confirm, Opened vs confirmed — and under Data Ops › Exports, Approvals Reports holds Hand-over receipts and Receipt lines; the email column needs View contact details, and the notes never leave the app.
