Release Notes

Changelog

Every update to GearLogs, described in plain language — newest first.

GL-33

Support Inside the App, and a Bell That Keeps You Posted

  • A Support section in the sidebar for every member: ask a question, report a problem, suggest an idea, or leave feedback, and follow the whole conversation in one thread — every request gets a short SUP code, your history stays with you, and administrators can see every request from their workspace
  • When we answer, the reply is in the app and in your email — the full text, with one link that opens that exact conversation. If a reply sits unopened for a few days, one reminder email follows; never more than one
  • Email stays a human door: write to support@gearlogs.com any time and a person answers from the mailbox. Nothing is machine-parsed from email into your workspace, so what is in the app is exactly what you and we wrote there
  • A bell in the header collects what happened while you were away — a new sign-in, gear assigned or returned, a write-off, a two-factor change, a billing event — and clicking an entry takes you to it. The Notifications tab holds the full list
  • Settings › Notifications lets each person choose, kind by kind, what arrives in the app and what also arrives by email; administrators set the workspace defaults. Security and billing essentials always arrive, and a clear warning appears if our emails to you are bouncing
  • Administrators get plain-language billing emails: subscription started, payment failed or recovered, seats changed, cycle switched, cancellation and resumption, invoices — plus reminders a week and a day before an evaluation or licence ends
  • Every notification email speaks your language — English or native Hebrew — following the language you chose for yourself in the app
GL-32

Search Everything, and a Theme That’s Yours

  • Search now finds everything: departments (click one and the board opens right on that tab), groups, team members, service contracts, and archived records — not just items and people
  • It matches on what you actually remember: reference codes, serial numbers, lot codes, designations and locations, manufacturer, model, category, SKU and GTIN, and the notes you wrote — archived records match just like active ones
  • Best matches come first with the matched text highlighted, and “+N more” carries the same search onto the screen you land on, so you keep narrowing instead of starting over
  • Your colour theme is now yours: pick it once and it follows you to any computer, without changing anyone else’s screen — administrators set the workspace default in Settings › Display, and one reset clears your personal choices
  • People’s names are accepted in any alphabet — Ibáñez, Traoré and Müller save as written — and phone numbers can be entered in any real-world format, international prefix included
GL-31

Transparent Billing

  • One plan with everything included: $29 per user per month, or $290 per user per year (two months free), minimum 5 seats — the same price on the pricing page, at checkout, and inside the app, with no tiers and no upsells
  • Settings gains a Billing area for admins: see your subscription at a glance, add or remove seats with the exact prorated amount shown before you confirm, switch between monthly and yearly, cancel at period end — and resume with one click if you change your mind — and download your invoices, all without leaving the app
  • Card details are handled entirely on the secure pages of Paddle, our merchant of record — GearLogs never sees or stores them
  • A dedicated billing log: every billing action — who changed seats, what the payment provider confirmed — is recorded and readable like the rest of your activity logs, in English or native Hebrew, searchable and exportable
  • Evaluation periods can be arranged without entering any payment details; when a subscription or evaluation ends, the workspace switches to read-only — your data stays visible and exportable, nothing is deleted
  • One sign-in at a time: signing in on a new device politely signs out the previous one, so a session forgotten open on another computer is never a risk
GL-30

One Consistent Tool

  • Every screen now uses the same pieces — the same table, the same buttons, the same search box — so the whole app feels like one tool, not a patchwork
  • Choose how busy your rows look: action buttons can be always shown or appear on hover, set to your own preference, with a workspace default an admin can set for everyone
  • Navigation finds things for you — jump straight from an item to its place on the board and back, and every long dropdown now has a search box, so there’s no more endless scrolling
  • Long names, serial numbers, emails, and locations are always fully readable — nothing is silently cut off with an ellipsis anymore
  • Hebrew reads natively throughout, right-to-left, with correct terminology — written in Hebrew, not translated into it
GL-29

Your Data, Your Reports

  • The Data area is now a proper workspace — Overview, Import, Reports, and Maintenance — instead of a vanity “100% healthy” score
  • The Overview runs real checks on your actual data (units missing serial numbers, gear still held by archived people, contracts covering nothing, and more), tells you plainly how many passed, and lets you jump straight to fix each one
  • A real report catalogue: reports across logistics, personnel, registry, archive, and activity — each exportable to CSV, Excel, or Print (Print is the reliable way to a clean PDF in Hebrew), and every report reads your complete records, so a large workspace exports in full instead of stopping at a hidden cap
  • An encrypted, whole-workspace backup you unlock with your own passphrase — and a backup file no longer carries anything it shouldn’t, like your member list or license key
  • If data is ever lost, recovery is done together with support — never by a single risky click inside the app
  • Settings now opens for every member: everyone can adjust their own display and see system status, while the workspace controls stay with admins
  • Archived service contracts now live in the Archive alongside retired people and equipment — restore one, or a super-admin can remove it for good
GL-28

Warranty & Service Contracts

  • GearLogs now tracks when coverage runs out — warranty dates on individual units and on whole lots, with one honest rule for which date applies
  • Coverage shows up everywhere as colour-coded chips (green, amber as it approaches, red once expired), an “Expiring” widget on your dashboard that lists the most urgent first, and a clock signal right on the board when a serialized item has units that need attention
  • You set the early-warning window yourself — how many days out counts as “expiring soon” — and every signal follows it
  • Full service-contract records — provider, coverage type, contract number, dates, and cost — attach to specific units or to a whole lot, so you always know exactly which gear each contract covers
  • Contract cost is visible to managers only, and every coverage-date change is kept as a tamper-proof history line
GL-27

Safer Moves, Cleaner Archive

  • Dragging an item or a person into a different group or tab now asks you to confirm first, so a stray click can’t quietly move something
  • When someone leaves, you can write off everything still in their hands and archive them in a single, confirmed step
  • Lost, damaged, or missing serialized units can now be written off individually and later recovered, with the full record kept
  • License-renewal reminders are clearer and calmer, and if a license ever lapses the workspace switches to read-only — nothing is lost
GL-26

Two-Factor Sign-In

  • Your workspace can now require two-factor authentication — members sign in with their password and a one-time code from an authenticator app
  • Two-factor can be required not just to sign in, but to make changes, so a stolen password on its own can’t alter your records
  • Everyone who turns it on gets a set of one-time recovery codes to keep in case they lose their phone
  • Admins manage the requirement for the whole workspace and can reset a member’s two-factor if they are ever locked out
  • It stays off until you choose to turn it on — nothing changes for your team until an admin switches it on
GL-25

Audited and Hardened

  • Before this release, GearLogs went through several rounds of independent expert review and an in-depth security check — here is what that means for you
  • Your records can’t be quietly tampered with: the history of who had what, and when, is protected and can’t be altered or forged by anyone who shouldn’t touch it
  • The numbers you see are honest — no item can be handed to two people at once, an action that fails tells you so instead of pretending it worked, and lost-gear counts never include gear you’ve since recovered
  • Nothing disappears silently: removing, importing, or reorganizing large amounts of data always reports exactly what happened, including anything it couldn’t do
  • Your backups are complete and safe to restore — a backup carries the full history, and GearLogs refuses to restore a file that has been truncated or altered
  • Everything reads correctly in Hebrew, right-to-left, throughout, and large workspaces stay fast and honest — when a list is too long to show at once, it says so rather than quietly cutting the rest
GL-24

Serial-Number Accountability

  • You can now track equipment unit by unit, not just by how many you have — each radio, sight, vehicle, or tool becomes its own numbered record with its own history
  • Every unit carries its own status, condition, and current holder, so you always know which one is issued, which is in for repair, and which is sitting in stock
  • Group units into lots or batches under a single item, and register them one at a time, in bulk, or by importing a spreadsheet
  • Condition has teeth: a unit marked unserviceable can’t be issued by mistake, and one that needs repair asks you to confirm before it goes out
  • Move a single unit between people, groups, or a repair bench — by dragging it or from a menu — and every hand-off is recorded automatically
  • Search by serial number, lot, or product and jump straight to the one unit you’re looking for

GL-17

Real Names, Your Own ID Field & Faster Handouts

  • Everyone is shown by their first and last name across the whole app — the user list, activity history, system logs, and analytics — each keeping their short reference code alongside
  • Record your own ID for each person — an employee number, staff ID, or service number — shown to administrators only, and you choose what the field is called so it matches how your organisation talks

GL-16

Delete Protection & a Consistent Look

  • Permanently erasing anything is now reserved for a Super Administrator — everyday admins keep running the workspace, but wiping a person, an item, a tab or group, or the loss history for good is limited to a super administrator, so nothing important is lost by accident. Every current administrator starts with this ability
  • Only a Super Administrator can grant or withdraw that power, and no one can give it to themselves — the safeguard is enforced behind the scenes and can't be worked around
  • Tighter control over shared areas — dashboard widgets can now only be changed by admins and operators, while everyone else can still see them, and removing a tab or group for good is limited to those allowed to
  • One consistent look for every label — statuses, reasons, and states now read as the same style of coloured tag across the whole app, and quantities show in a matching boxed style, so screens are quicker to scan and line up with the reference codes and activity log
  • Names where you expect them — the loss and personnel dashboard views now show a person's name next to their code, instead of the code alone
  • The same search and filters on every Archive tab — retired people, archived equipment, and write-offs each get the same search, filters, a clear Reset button, and export

More Ways to Track Loss

  • Record gear that goes missing or gets damaged on the shelf — before it's ever handed to anyone — so storeroom losses are tracked too, not just gear a person is holding
  • Undo a write-off that shouldn't have happened — the item goes straight back into that person's hands and the loss is cancelled, while the record stays on file marked as voided
  • Write off several of a person's items in one step when more than one thing is lost or damaged, and keep the person on the roster
  • A new dashboard view ranks who has lost the most gear
  • Open a focused loss history for any retired person or archived item straight from the Archive — exactly what was lost, why, and when
