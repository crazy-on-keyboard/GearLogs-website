Release Notes

Changelog

Every update to GearLogs, described in plain language — newest first.

GL-51

Clean Records, by Rule

  • A staff record follows one rule set wherever it is written — the form, a CSV import and the workspace itself agree, so a record that saves is a record that is clean
  • Names in any script up to 100 characters; ranks and departments up to 60; an email that looks like an email; a phone that is a phone; notes up to 500 characters
  • No hidden or invisible characters can enter a record — pasted text is checked, and the refusal says which field and why, in your language
  • A CSV row that breaks a record rule stops the import before any record is written — the refusal names the row and the field (“Row 12: email — …”); rows set aside for review (a duplicate, a missing name) wait in the conflicts queue as before
GL-50

Who Sees What

  • Staff contact details — phone, email, notes — are now a right of their own, held by administrators and operators, and grantable to any other person; everyone else sees a small lock in their place — and a member who cannot see a detail can never overwrite it
  • Any right in the list except Manage Users (administrators alone) can be added to a person on top of their role — and a role’s own rights are never taken away
  • Archived people, gear, lots, units and contracts are handed only to members who can open the Archive; search follows the same rule, and the other boards stay live
  • Renaming a location, rank or department now reaches every record carrying the old name, archived ones included
  • A colleague’s login, rights and personal settings are private to them — and the Users screen names every right in Hebrew too
GL-49

What You See Is Current

  • A saved dashboard widget changes on its card the moment you see “Widget saved” — no more waiting for the board to catch up
  • The bell never shows a false zero: when your unread count could not be checked it shows a dash, says so to a screen reader, and tries again within a minute
  • If the consent screen appears over your work — after a save was refused until you accept the current terms — accepting returns you to the screen you were on, not the home board
GL-48

Consent, Enforced

  • The workspace itself refuses any change made under a person’s name until they have accepted the current Terms of Service and Privacy Policy — stock, people, settings, membership and the activity log alike
  • Support requests stay open, so you can always reach us
  • If a save is ever refused for this reason, the consent screen appears at once and says the change was not saved
  • When a new version of the terms is published, everyone is signed out to a screen that says so, and reviews the new version at the next sign-in
  • An acceptance, once given, is final
GL-47

Widgets That Keep Their Size

  • A dashboard card keeps the size you gave it; a long list scrolls inside the card instead of stretching the page
  • Every list widget offers “Show top” — All, 3, 5, 12 or 25 — next to the charts’ existing top-N
  • The Data Recovery card opens a support request already filled in with your workspace and the checklist — nothing to compose by e-mail
  • When the sign-in security check fails or expires, the screen says so and refreshes it, instead of blaming your password

A Demo That Shows Everything

  • Serialised units in every state with their custody history; lots with expiry dates; warranties running out; service contracts covering units and whole lots
  • Write-offs, some recovered; an archive; storage capacity with a few products already over their ceiling
  • Product details down to SKU and manufacturer; people with contact details, team heads and secondary departments
  • A quarter of activity, a ready-made dashboard, and the bell alerts the rules would have raised
  • Six sectors — Office, Medical, IT, Army (Hebrew-flavoured), Logistics, Manufacturing — landing in one step, everything or nothing, and only in an empty workspace
GL-45

End the Demo, Start Clean

  • End the demo clears the whole workspace back to empty in one step — the sample data and everything anyone added while exploring
  • Before it runs, the dialog shows exactly what goes, with counts, and you choose whether the other users, the workspace settings, your support conversations and the error reports stay — all kept unless you tick them
  • Billing, your licence and the workspace name are never touched
  • Take an encrypted backup first, type a word to confirm, and everyone is signed out; the next sign-in lands on a clean workspace offering Load a demo, Import data or a new tab
  • Reference codes and workspace ids are system-owned, never editable; a private widget is edited or deleted only by its owner
GL-44

Columns That Stack, Groups You Size

  • Edit Layout on the Board, like the dashboard: move a group earlier or later with one click

GL-43

GL-42

Counted, Not Sampled

  • Day, week and month buckets follow your own timezone; a week runs Monday to Sunday
  • Group cards on the Board are counted on the server too — one call for the whole board
  • The log table stays a list — the newest 200 rows of the period

Who Sees a Widget

  • The Board’s group cards read at a glance: stock as a ring (in stock · in use · in repair), activity as stacked columns coloured by type over 30, 60 or 90 days — your choice, remembered — and who has the gear out, by department

  • Only charts the source can honestly show are offered; a chart that needs a grouping cannot be saved without one

How Much Can We Hold

  • Adding stock past the line asks first — it never refuses
  • Capacity reaches the Registry list, both inventory reports, CSV import, the history line, a health check and three dashboard widgets — fill, headroom and over capacity
  • Administrators are told the moment an item goes over its capacity or runs low — in the bell, and by email if they choose
  • Every ring chart reads without hovering: a legend with counts sits beside the ring, and product rows show the name next to the code
GL-36

Green Gives, Red Takes

  • The confirmation behind an action wears the action’s colour, so the promise holds one click deeper — and a reversible action such as Send to repair keeps its own icon rather than a delete mark
  • Every help legend shows exactly the colours its buttons wear, in English and in Hebrew, in every theme
  • Restoring a person, item or contract from the Archive now asks first
  • The Archive shows the same “your permissions here” card as every other screen
  • Every button and link shows a hand cursor
GL-35

Help That Is Always in the Same Place

  • Green puts things back, red takes them away: Restore, Recover and Back from repair are green on every screen and in every legend, and their confirmation dialogs match

  • A round of polish from an independent review: the first load is faster, the System Logs filter shows exactly the rows it says, Hebrew screens keep dates and codes the right way round, the confirmation word you type is in your language, and a version update while the app is open reloads quietly instead of showing a technical error

  • The thinking behind it is in the field note Software that explains itself
GL-34

Forgot Your Password? Sort It Yourself

  • Reset your password from the sign-in screen: an 8-digit code arrives by email and you type it into the same screen — no link to click, nothing for a mail filter to break, and no waiting for an administrator. The code works for one hour and only once
  • Every member can change their password any time from Settings › Security. Your current password is asked for first, and the check is enforced by the platform, not just the screen
  • Settings › Security is now every member’s: your own two-factor and your recovery codes live there too, while the workspace-wide two-factor requirement stays with administrators
  • Every password change signs out every other device and browser, and confirms itself with a security email and a bell notice
  • Two-factor stays in force: a login that uses it must confirm it before a reset completes
  • GearLogs stays invite-only — there is no public sign-up
GL-33

Support Inside the App, and a Bell That Keeps You Posted

  • A Support section in the sidebar for every member: ask a question, report a problem, suggest an idea, or leave feedback, and follow the whole conversation in one thread — every request gets a short SUP code, your history stays with you, and administrators can see every request from their workspace
  • When we answer, the reply is in the app and in your email — the full text, with one link that opens that exact conversation. If a reply sits unopened for a few days, one reminder email follows; never more than one
  • Email stays a human door: write to support@gearlogs.com any time and a person answers from the mailbox. Nothing is machine-parsed from email into your workspace, so what is in the app is exactly what you and we wrote there
  • A bell in the header collects what happened while you were away — a new sign-in, gear assigned or returned, a write-off, a two-factor change, a billing event — and clicking an entry takes you to it. The Notifications tab holds the full list
  • Settings › Notifications lets each person choose, kind by kind, what arrives in the app and what also arrives by email; administrators set the workspace defaults. Security and billing essentials always arrive, and a clear warning appears if our emails to you are bouncing
  • Administrators get plain-language billing emails: subscription started, payment failed or recovered, seats changed, cycle switched, cancellation and resumption, invoices — plus reminders a week and a day before an evaluation or licence ends
  • Every notification email speaks your language — English or native Hebrew — following the language you chose for yourself in the app

GL-25

Audited and Hardened

  • Before this release, GearLogs went through several rounds of independent expert review and an in-depth security check — here is what that means for you
  • Your records can’t be quietly tampered with: the history of who had what, and when, is protected and can’t be altered or forged by anyone who shouldn’t touch it
  • The numbers you see are honest — no item can be handed to two people at once, an action that fails tells you so instead of pretending it worked, and lost-gear counts never include gear you’ve since recovered
